# Legal & compliance

synthteam processes data about **real, identifiable people** — your colleagues —
to build profiles of how they think and decide. Depending on where you and they
are based, that may be regulated by data-protection, privacy, and employment
law.

**Understanding and complying with the law that applies to you is your
responsibility.** This project does not and cannot do that for you. Before using
synthteam against named individuals, satisfy yourself that doing so is lawful in
the relevant jurisdictions — and if you are unsure, get advice from a qualified
lawyer.

## For informational purposes only

The items below are examples of the kinds of law that *can* be relevant when
profiling people from workplace communications. They are listed for general
awareness only. This is **not legal advice**, the list is **not exhaustive**,
and none of it is a substitute for understanding the rules that actually apply
to your situation.

- **EU/EEA — data protection.** [General Data Protection Regulation
  (GDPR)](https://gdpr-info.eu/) — building profiles of identifiable people is
  regulated processing.
- **Germany — employee representation.** [Works Constitution Act
  (Betriebsverfassungsgesetz / BetrVG)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_betrvg/)
  — co-determination can apply to workplace tools.
- **Finland — employee privacy.** [Act on the Protection of Privacy in Working
  Life (759/2004)](https://www.finlex.fi/en/legislation/translations/2004/eng/759)
  — restricts processing of employee data.
- **EU — AI regulation.** [EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/) —
  rules for AI systems, including some used in an employment context.
- **New Zealand — privacy.** [Privacy Act
  2020](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/0031/latest/whole.html).

Many other jurisdictions have comparable laws. Treat the above as a prompt to
investigate, not a checklist.
